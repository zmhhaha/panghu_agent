# Express the production explanation

1. Read the exact scene, real audio clock and complete offered methods. Identify
   what viewers must understand at each narrated beat, including dependencies,
   changing states and relationships. Preserve factual meaning.
2. Choose an original visual mechanism that reveals that explanation over time.
   Optional methods can supply a reasoning technique; they do not prescribe a
   fixed diagram, relabeled demo or hard-coded component arrangement. Reject
   irrelevant methods and adapt useful ones to this scene's actual structure.
3. Compose a complete expressive passage. Make important changes occur when
   the corresponding idea is spoken. Use motion to reveal object identity,
   causality, changing quantities or spatial relationships. Avoid filler title
   cards, premature connectors and decorative movement that obscures meaning.
   Keep labels readable and preserve useful visual context across the passage.
4. Author original source against absolute time. Stage objects and relationships
   deliberately; do not expose later states before their narrated explanation.
   Honor explicit revision feedback while preserving the current audio clock.
5. Return one precise apply/adapt/reject decision for every offered version.
   Give source targets only where you actually authored a corresponding idea.
   Record genuine limitations and tradeoffs. Neither your declaration nor a
   source substring is evidence of observed rendering or aesthetic success.

No narration synthesis or playback is part of this authoring task. It receives
the recording clock as trusted business input; isolated rendering and a later
viewer review determine what was actually presented.

Choose the rendering medium from the exact admitted allowed_engines subset,
according to the task rather than a fixed template. HTML/SVG can express layout
and relationships; Canvas2D can draw dense fields or marks; Three.js can clarify
spatial structure. These are options, never a requirement to use a complex
engine. Keep the confirmed narration, measured clock and method delivery intact.
For Canvas/Three, recompute the entire visible state from absolute seconds and
render each sought frame. Initialize deterministically, await bounded readiness,
and avoid autonomous animation, frame accumulation or external assets.


Before authoring, read `film_context.film` from beginning to end. Locate this
passage on the complete measured timeline and understand what the preceding
passage established and what the following one must explain. If predecessor
is present, reconcile its exact source summary and handoff with the current
passage. Preserve semantic object IDs for continuing subjects; use consistent
appearance and an intentional state/view transfer. Avoid a reset title card or
independent slide when the argument can continue as one expressive passage.
The full-film context guides creative choices without prescribing a layout.

After authoring, describe the actual code's entry and exit, view and semantic
objects in the bounded result handoff. Anchor each declared object in real
returned source. Plan the exit to support the next passage's purpose and make
changes of view/scale legible. Do not claim you observed a render or that your
self-report is verified continuity. The later renderer and reviewer must check
actual neighboring frames and playback.
